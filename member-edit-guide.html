Style Guide

Thanks for taking the time to help keep the scorecard accurate. Every edit you submit is reviewed by ASVA before it goes live; nothing you write is published automatically; following this guide makes it far more likely your suggestion is accepted as-is, and quickly.

1. Source every claim

The scorecard's credibility rests on every claim being checkable. For each bullet point you add or change, include a source; this could be a link to the MP's own statement, a vote record, a written question, a campaign signatory list, or comparable public record. A bullet with no source is the single most common reason a submission is sent back, so this alone will save you a round trip.

2. Keep it factual

  • Describe what the MP has said or done ("Voted against the Bill on 12 March 2026"), not how you feel about it ("Ian Byrne clearly doesn't care about AI safety").
  • Avoid campaigning language, sarcasm, or personal characterisations of the MP. The scorecard grades a voting/public record, not a personality.
  • If something is genuinely ambiguous or contested, say so; "unclear" or "no public statement found" is a more honest bullet than a confident guess.

3. Follow the format

  • Keep each bullet to one clear sentence; the card has room for up to five.
  • Up to six sources per MP; give each one a short, plain title (e.g. "Written question, 30 May 2025") rather than pasting the raw URL as the title.
  • Only propose a grade change if the bullets you're submitting genuinely support a different grade than the one currently shown; the grade should follow from the evidence, not the other way round.

4. Grading rubric

Use this as a rough guide for which grade the evidence supports:

GradeWhat it looks like
ASigned a campaign statement (ControlAI or PauseAI) and shown active governance work, e.g. led a Westminster Hall or Commons debate on AI safety, tabled an EDM or written questions on AI risk, sponsored a relevant bill, or repeatedly spoken publicly on catastrophic risk.
BClear positive alignment but lighter, e.g. a meaningful PauseAI or ControlAI signatory but little else on record.
CSome positive engagement, or a general push for AI regulation.
DLeans against safety governance, e.g. prioritises "innovation" or "growth" over safeguards, or is dismissive of AI safety without a hard record of active opposition.
EActively works against AI safety, e.g. voted or campaigned against binding regulation, an AI Bill, or independent oversight.
FVocal opposition to AI safety, e.g. calls AI risk "hype" or "doomerism", champions deregulation or an "AI arms race".

5. What not to submit

  • Unsourced claims, rumour, or anything you can't point to a public record for.
  • Personal attacks, or claims about the MP's motives rather than their actions.
  • Duplicate submissions for minor wording tweaks; if you need to fix something in an edit you already sent, just submit again; your new version replaces the pending one automatically.

What happens after you submit

An ASVA reviewer checks your suggested edit against the sources you provided. You'll get an email either way: if it's published, whether it went live exactly as you wrote it or with some changes; if it isn't, a short note on why not so you can try again.
