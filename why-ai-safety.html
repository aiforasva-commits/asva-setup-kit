Why AI Safety

Find out why growing numbers want action.

1. AI is approaching human level

You've probably noticed AI getting sharper, but the pace is easy to underestimate. Systems now write code, pass professional exams, and outperform most people at tasks we had assumed needed human judgement.

2. AI development is a race

Whoever develops the first truly human level AI will control the economy, so the leading labs are locked into a flat-out race. Worse, many labs have the explicit goal of building an AI beyond human intelligence.

3. We can't control advanced AI

No one, not even the people building these systems, knows how to control an AI at human level, let alone one smarter than us. We're barely in control of the AI we already have.

4. Uncontrolled AI could cause extinction

Geoffrey Hinton, who won a Nobel Prize for his work on AI, left Google specifically to warn about it. In 2023 top experts around the world, including the heads of major AI labs, signed a one-line statement putting AI extinction risk alongside pandemics and nuclear war.

5. There are no guardrails

There is no law against a company building a dangerous AI system. There is not even a legal requirement for these systems to be tested before release. Despite this, right now AI safety barely registers on the political radar.

6. Safety is possible

None of this is hopeless. There are real proposals that can work; testing regimes, oversight rules, international agreements. History shows that we can hold back dangerous technology when society makes this a priority. ASVA exists to make AI safety a priority for our society – and for the people who govern it.
